01 / Evaluation

Decide what needs attention.

A human evaluation can be discussed as a separate engagement from software-based AI reviews and final imprint consideration. Explain the questions you need answered: structure, argument, audience, voice, or readiness.

Before agreeing, confirm which material will be read, what feedback will be delivered, whether a conversation is included, and the expected timing. Evaluation is not automatically an edit.

02 / Editing

Give the revision a purpose.

Describe the state of the manuscript and the kind of intervention you need. Structural work, sentence-level attention, and final checking involve different responsibilities. Ask which are included in the proposed service.

Agree review rounds, author approvals, handoff formats, and how changes outside the scope are handled. Keep the author’s role in decisions explicit. A productive engagement depends on shared expectations as much as on expertise.

03 / Ghostwriting

Bring expertise into a coherent book.

If your knowledge or experience is the starting point, discuss the material you can supply and the role you want a writer to play. Interviews, research inputs, existing notes, and draft review should be considered in the scope.

Agree responsibilities for factual verification, permissions, authorship, confidentiality, and approvals in the actual engagement. No fixed package, price, or turnaround is promised on this page.

04 / Managed production

Coordinate the work beyond the draft.

Discuss the design and production tasks your edition needs. Ask about interiors, cover preparation, file checking, revisions, and any third-party costs. A finished cover image alone is not a complete production plan.

Authors can commission support while pursuing independent publication. Professional-service work does not, by itself, grant acceptance under the 36seas imprint. Availability, deliverables, costs, and rights belong in your separate agreement.
