Self-publishing or a publishing partner?

By 36Seas Publishing · Updated September 22, 2026

Choose based on the work you want to own, the help you need, and the scope you can agree in writing. Software access and a publishing service solve different problems.

When does self-publishing make sense?

It suits authors who want to coordinate the project and manage their publishing accounts. You may still hire specialists for individual tasks. Start with a list of remaining work: manuscript revision, editing, interior layout, cover production, file checks, and release preparation.

When should I consider a publishing partner?

Consider professional support when you need help coordinating agreed editorial or production work. Ask for specific deliverables, review rounds, milestones, exclusions, and author responsibilities. A description such as “full service” is not a substitute for a written scope.

What should I compare before paying?

  • Who supplies and approves each file, and in which formats?
  • Who controls publishing accounts and maintains release information?
  • Which tasks, revisions, and third-party costs are included?
  • What rights and obligations does the agreement establish?
  • What happens if a manuscript needs more work or is not accepted?

Publishing and distribution are different decisions

A distribution arrangement describes where a book can be offered and who manages that arrangement. Ask which formats and channels are actually covered. Do not treat availability as a promise of bookstore stocking or sales.
