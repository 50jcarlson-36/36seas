Independent publication, professional support, and an imprint relationship solve different problems. Compare responsibilities as carefully as benefits.

01 / Independent publication

You coordinate the release.

Use First Mate to develop and prepare the manuscript, then export within your paid-plan allowances. You decide where to publish, manage the retailer accounts, check files, and maintain the release information.

Independence does not prevent you from hiring an editor or production specialist. It means the overall publishing decisions and coordination remain with you unless you explicitly agree otherwise.

02 / Professional support

You commission defined work.

Human evaluation, editing, ghostwriting, or managed production can address specific needs. Ask for deliverables, rounds, milestones, exclusions, and author responsibilities. A broad phrase such as “full service” cannot replace a scope.

Clarify who supplies each file, who checks it, and who approves the result. Discuss third-party costs and work outside the original scope before a change becomes a surprise.

03 / Imprint publication

You seek a publishing relationship.

The 36seas path involves separate paid manuscript review, editorial consideration, and—if selected—a separate agreement. Software membership is not a purchase of publication.

The agreement defines services, costs, rights, and release responsibilities. Distribution describes where a book can be offered and who manages the arrangement; it is not a guarantee of bookstore stocking or sales.

04 / Choose deliberately

There is more than one successful outcome.

Choose the path that matches the work you want to manage, the support you need, and the relationship you are prepared to enter. Exporting your finished book is a valid First Mate outcome. So is preparing it for imprint consideration.

Start with your current manuscript rather than an abstract label. If it needs development, work on the draft. If it is finished, compare the next steps on their actual commitments.
