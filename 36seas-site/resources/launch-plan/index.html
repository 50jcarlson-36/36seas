The 36seas field notes

A book ready to meet
its next reader.

Build a launch around a clear reason to read and a reliable destination. Start with the essentials you can maintain.

On this page

PositionDestinationAssetsFollow-through

01 / Position

Say what the reader is being invited into.

Write a short description that makes the book’s premise or purpose clear. Identify the reader most likely to recognize its value. Use the same core promise across the book page, retailer description, and launch communication.

Keep the language specific to the work. A meaningful premise, relevant problem, or distinctive perspective gives a reader more to evaluate than a broad claim that a book will change everything.

02 / Destination

Make the next action obvious.

Choose the purchase destination for each promotion. For the 36seas catalog, book-buying links lead to Amazon. Check the listing, available edition, and destination before sending people to it.

Separate a purchase invitation from an author signup. A reader who wants a book should not need to join a software funnel first. An author curious about your process can discover that path through a contextual link.

03 / Assets

Prepare a small coherent set.

Gather the approved cover, concise description, author biography, and any sample or supporting material you have permission to share. Keep title and author details consistent. Check that images remain legible at the sizes where they will appear.

Use genuine endorsements only with permission and accurate attribution. Do not promise reviews, rankings, or sales. If a trailer or event is still upcoming, label it that way rather than giving it the appearance of a completed asset.

04 / Follow-through

Plan what happens after the announcement.

Think about reader questions, appearances, follow-up content, and how you will maintain links over time. A launch is more useful when it creates a continuing place for the book rather than a single burst of attention.

Measure what you can actually observe. An outbound retailer click signals interest; it is not automatically a sale. Use verified reporting when available and keep interpretation proportional to the evidence.
