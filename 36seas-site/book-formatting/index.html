Prepare each edition deliberately. Formatting is the bridge between your manuscript and the way a reader encounters it.

01 / Choose an edition

Start with the destination’s requirements.

Decide whether you are preparing print, digital, or both. Consult the current requirements of your chosen publishing platform for its accepted files and production specifications. This guide is a planning checklist, not a substitute for those requirements.

Keep edition choices together: trim size, interior treatment, image use, and cover requirements. Some decisions affect one another. A change in the interior can change page count and therefore the cover spine specifications.

02 / Interior

Inspect more than the opening page.

Review front matter, chapter openings, headings, paragraph spacing, page breaks, images, captions, and back matter. Check any table of contents and internal links where applicable. Look at the beginning, middle, and end of the exported book.

For digital editions, check reading behavior on appropriate devices or preview tools. For print, inspect margins, image quality, pagination, and any elements that approach the trim. A proof or platform preview can reveal problems a manuscript view cannot.

03 / Cover

Treat the wrap as a production file.

Front cover, spine, and back cover must work together for the intended edition. Confirm final dimensions and the information required by the publishing platform. Keep important text readable and positioned appropriately.

A cover concept is not automatically a finished production asset. Inspect typography, author name, title, and any back-cover copy. Recheck the wrap after interior changes that affect the page count.

04 / Approval

Record the exact files you release.

Use a clear file-naming approach and retain the approved files for each edition. After correcting a problem, inspect the replacement rather than assuming the change affected only one page.

First Mate’s paid plans include formatting exports within their allowances. Confirm the available output for your project in the application. Authors publishing independently manage final checks and retailer submission; human production support is a separate service.
